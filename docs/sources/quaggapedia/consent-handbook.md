**STAR** aims to prevent Sexual and Gender-based Violence (SGBV) within the AfrikaBurn community and beyond. AfrikaBurn has a zero-tolerance policy for STAR incidents and makes **Enthusiastic Consent Culture** (see CONSENT) and **Community Safety** an absolute priority.

A consent violation is **any activity without clear, voluntary, and ongoing consent**, and **can also extend to touching, emotional manipulation, harassment or creating an unsafe atmosphere.**

### **Some types of consent violations:**

- ***Sexual harassment:*** any words, gestures or behaviours intended to be sexual which are unwelcome and/or without consent. *Examples* include making sexual jokes, sexual teasing or comments on appearance, overstepping boundaries or overly close contact.
- ***Sexual assault and/or rape:*** any contact on or with a sexual part of the body, or any contact made in a sexual way anywhere on the body that is unwanted and/or without consent. *Examples of sexual assault and/or rape:* include being made to touch someone or being touched, hugging to be gratified, intruding into body-energy space without consent. Being shown sexual content or body parts without consent. Threats of any of these.
- ***Physical violations***: Non-sexual physical interactions that are **unwanted, such as unwanted hugging or physical intimidation.**
- ***Emotional and psychological breaches***: **Behavior that undermines an individual's autonomy, such as manipulation and gaslighting.**
- ***Digital misconduct:*** Taking photos without consent. Sharing private images or messages without consent, especially if intimate or threats to. Cyberstalking, doxing or digital harassment.

### **The 5 key elements of consent are:**

- Consent is **freely given**.
- Consent is **informed by understandable, true and correct information**.
- The **consent is specific**.
- The **consent is current**.
- Consent (the **authentic feeling of "yes"**) is clear to the person, and is communicated clearly.

**'Consent is not something that one person gets from another, in order to do something to them.**
**Consent is an equal, clear agreement between two people with mutual respect, about what they both want.**
Consent is an **active and shared understanding**. It's **not passive**. It's **not the absence of a no**. If you're guessing, hoping or assuming you're not in consent.

If you don't have a clear, shared understanding you don't yet have consent and you're not in consent together.

**'When it comes to sex and consent, consent is not permission from one person to another to do what you want to do... consent is a shared experience where both of you know what that experience will include...**
**Consent is taking ownership of what you want, and having a direct conversation on whether you both want to experience that together, in that moment.**

**Examples:**

*Q: "I would like to kiss you right now... would you like that too?"*

*A: "Yes/maybe/can I have more info/no"*

### **5 Key elements of good consent conversations:**

1. **Make a direct, specific request** that isn't a yes or no answer
2. **Notice** words, vibes, sounds AND body language - these are all the answer
3. **Listen to your gut** - don't ignore weird feelings - you need more clarity
4. **Accept** any "No or Maybe" type words and vibes with appreciation.
5. As the experience changes, your **shared agreements should match the changes** for e.g kissing changes to touching, update the agreement.

Things change. So can your mind, and so should your expressed consent.

**Consent can be withdrawn** whenever you want it to be - an agreement needs more than one person - **even in the middle of something** you thought you wanted to share, but now don't.

## Authentic consent is the new enthusiastic consent - either way the absence of a "no" is not consent.
A consensual experience will include **mutual, active participation and free expression** between people of who wants what and how as it happens.

**Enthusiastic consent looks like:**

- Saying *"Yes, I want to"* or *"I'm into this"*
- **Smiling, nodding, or engaging** in a **relaxed and comfortable** way
- Checking in with questions like, *"Are you still okay with"*

### Where consent is not present:

- **Ignoring** or **disregarding** a no instead of stopping or making a shared consent agreement
- exerting your energy to **manipulate, manoeuvre, or manhandle** your way into sexually gratifying yourself.
- Justifying not having consent based on how you want to interpret clothing, flirting or past encounters instead of making a shared consent agreement.
- Intimidating, pressuring, threatening, or guilting someone to get an inauthentic yes instead of consent
- Taking advantage of someone who is too intoxicated to prevent you from acting without consent.
- Taking advantage of silence or a shutdown state (a lack of strong physical or verbal resistance) to ignore consent, and act without it.

Consent can't be assumed. **Consent** by definition **is a clear, freely given, authentically felt and expressed** shared agreement. Aren't experiencing that? It's not consent!

**Consent isn't a set of techniques or questions that you check off so that you can gratify yourself without resistance.**

*Are you dancing if someone is just dragging you around a dancefloor by your arm?*

*Are you boxing if someone on the street walks past you and punches you suddenly?*

*Are you having sex if someone is having sex with your body as they see fit?*

### Signs of having experienced a boundary violation...
**Emotional and mental signs**

- Feeling resentful, guilty, or anxious: These feelings can arise when you consistently go against your own desires to please others.
- Loss of self or values: You may feel a sense of having lost your own identity or values because you've given them away to accommodate others.
- Feeling responsible for others' emotions: This can manifest as constantly trying to manage other people's feelings or happiness and feeling anxious when you have your own needs.
- Feeling pressured: You may feel a constant push to do things you don't want to do, and making decisions for yourself is difficult.
- Discomfort or an "off" feeling: A persistent sense of unease is a strong indicator that something is wrong.
- You aren't very good at knowing what you want (or you think you're easy and don't want much), or can't easily feel your feelings, and express what you need.

**Behavioural signs**

- The other person continually ignores or challenges your requests and limits.
- You're frequently in a cycle of saying yes to something you don't want to do and then regretting it later.
- Your relationship is characterised by an unhealthy reliance on the other person, rather than a balanced partnership.
- Invalidating your feelings: The other person dismisses or minimizes your feelings, making you doubt your own reactions.
- Walking on eggshells to avoid explosions or arguments, silence and sulking or getting a backlash.

**Physical signs**

- Unwanted touch: This can range from tickling or hugging to more severe acts that cross your physical boundaries.
- Invasion of privacy: Someone might go through your things, check your phone, or share your personal information without permission.
- Restriction of autonomy: they limit your movements, isolate you from others, monitor what you do, make you feel like you can't be trusted, or they need you too much for you to take space.

## Consent & AfrikaBurn Principles

### **Radical Inclusion:**
- Everyone should be able to feel like they belong and everyone should feel safe.

### **Consensual Gifting**
- Being upfront about what the gift is, example: *'will it put me into an altered state?"* *'I'd like that brownie but not at the expense of my sobriety'.*
- *For gifts like yoga classes, if you are going to make adjustments through touch, ensure you ask for consent beforehand and that you have a consent agent in the space OR better yet, opt for verbal adjustments only.*
- Ensuring that gifts such as massages, etc. are within the consent boundaries laid out by the person receiving the gift.

### **Radical Self Expression**
- Every person should be able to express themselves freely without worrying about attracting unwanted attention such as being heckled or harassed.
- Also, ask for consent before taking photos.

### **Communal Effort**
- Everyone in our community is responsible for creating spaces that are safe and consensual.

### **Immediacy**
- Trust your instincts,
- talk to each other,
- report red flags,
- Do something in the moment.

### **Each One Teach One**
- Teaching and learning about consent is an ongoing practice which can also empower each of us to step in.

### **Leave No Trace**
- Take responsibility for the energy, intentions and actions you bring to the burn. Leave no negative trace on other people, or the places you go.

### **Civic Responsibility**
- Everyone in our community is empowered to intervene where someone seems to feel or is unsafe.

## Here's some low-risk ways to intervene that can have a powerful impact on someone else's safety and well-being:

### **^C: CREATE a Distraction**
A small distraction can halt harmful intentions by providing crucial moments for someone to reach safety.

### **^A: ASK Directly**
**Trust your instincts**. If someone looks uncomfortable or unsafe, checking in can change the outcome dramatically. Ask: *"Do you need help?" / "Do you want me to stay with you?" / "Would you like to leave with me and go somewhere safe?"*

### **^R: RALLY Others**
- Ask a friend or trusted person to help.
- Inform the Rangers who are trained to handle these situations.
- If things feel unsafe call for help from Rangers.

### **^E: EXTEND Support**
- Offer to stay with them or escort them safely to their camp or to Sanctuary.
- Listen with compassion. Your non-judgmental support can significantly impact their recovery.
