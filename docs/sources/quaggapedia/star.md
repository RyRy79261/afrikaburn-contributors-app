**WHAT IS AFRIKABURN'S STAR ALL ABOUT?**

STAR refers to the AfrikaBurn Safety Trust Awareness and Response initiative, which aims to prevent Sexual and Gender-based Violence (SGBV) within the AfrikaBurn community and beyond. AfrikaBurn has a zero-tolerance policy for STAR incidents and makes Enthusiastic Consent Culture and Community Safety an absolute priority.

We know that women and girls, especially, are targets for sexual violence in their homes and neighbourhoods, as well as clubs, parties and festivals. AfrikaBurn aims to 'invent the world anew' despite being a community of ordinary people with everyday issues, and not a Utopia. We realised that even though we too need to grapple with long-standing, complex social issues, we could still DO something new: by thinking, behaving and responding differently. And by applying our principles.

**A LITTLE BIT OF STAR HISTORY**

The STAR initiative was formed in 2018 in response to a desire to address both actual and potential sexual harassment in Tankwa Town. We began by developing a guiding policy to establish the AfrikaBurn standard of conduct: creating a positive, caring, and consent-based culture, and addressing misconduct within the community and the organisation.

Developing a policy that covered working, volunteering and living together in a remote location at a culture-bending arts festival was not easy. Research on SGBV policies pointed to wildly different types of documents used in wildly different settings: everything from university campuses, to NGOs, to neighbourhoods, to churches (and other religious groups), to workplaces.

The AfrikaBurn location shares a lot of the challenges from these divergent settings, such as partying and alcohol use, shared living spaces (eg like campus policy), power dynamics and social roles (like communities, NGOs and churches), but also the much stricter regulations, duties and safety laws of workplace and employment policies. Eventually, the social, legal, and ethical principles were integrated to cover *most* situations, and the policy was launched. It has been used, tested and adapted every year since.

**WHAT IS IN THE STAR POLICY?**

The STAR policy covers all emergency and non-emergency boundary and consent violations in which sexual harassment, incapacitation or coercion, intimate partner violence, or sexual violence is attempted or occurs. The policy is used to guide the making, handling, and resolution of complaints of this nature, and to ensure compliance with "good practice" standards and South African law. The policy principles encompass the making, investigation, and handling of complaints, as well as actions to ensure complainant safety and support, and direct fair and ethical decision-making, care, sanctions, and resolution. It also sets out the necessary steps to follow to protect privacy and confidentiality throughout.

The STAR Team is a small group of trained professionals and/or dedicated, appropriately appointed AfrikaBurn employees with an intersectional and person-centred approach. Together they: a) respond immediately on site to handle incidents and provide immediate care, b) protect privacy, c) make technical and evidence-based recommendations to inform organisational decision-making and resource allocation, d) work to develop effective interventions.

**HOW STAR MIGHT BENEFIT OTHERS**

AfrikaBurn's STAR Policy has been developed as an open-source resource, because creating safer spaces is something that will benefit all communities, not just our own. We actively encourage other events, festivals, and organisations to use, adapt, and remix this policy to suit their own contexts. By sharing these tools openly, we hope to spark a culture of accountability, care, and safety across South Africa and beyond. Together, we can build communities and spaces where everyone feels safe, respected, supported, and free to express themselves.

### Who is STAR?

STAR is the team that works on community safety and self-expression, through consent and community care when we gather.

### How do I call STAR when I need them?

![Safe Space2.png](https://quaggapedia.afrikaburn.com/index.php?title=Special:Redirect/file/Safe%20Space2.png)

* Ask a *ranger* or ranger HQ to radio STAR, and we will meet you there
* Ask at *sanctuary* (next to rangers) to radio STAR, and we will come to you
* Ask any *event guardian* for help - they can also radio STAR, or they will go with you to a safe place where we can meet you
* Go to the *site office* and ask the guardian at the bus gate, or the *VOC* (venue control room) to call STAR. We will meet you there
