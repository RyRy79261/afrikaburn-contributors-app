Participants needed:
To fill our vibrant space with more artistic expression, freedom, and fun we are looking for YOU to contribute with your personal artistic magic. Our camp embodies the spirit of collaboration, inviting participants to immerse themselves in a dynamic world of movement, sound, and art. Whether you come to dance, create, move or simply connect, you’ll find that Bee-Have isn’t just a space—it’s an experience, leaving visitors with a renewed sense of belonging and purpose. We are looking for artists, entertainers, workshop facilitators, yoga/pilates/breathwork trainers, bands, dancers, singers, poets, …. who are interested to participate in our program. 
## Project Description:
Discover the buzzing energy of BeeHave, a theme camp that celebrates community, creativity, and connection at AfrikaBurn 2026. Step into a vibrant hive of artistic expression, freedom, and fun. Let your spirit take flight as you join us in our hexagonal oasis, designed to inspire and delight. BeeHave isn’t just a space, it’s an experience, where everyone can contribute to the magic, leaving with a renewed sense of belonging and purpose.
We’re a group of humans who care about:
  * Making a warm, welcoming camp that isn’t plug-and-play.
  * Sharing food, fire, art and play in a way that feels held and resourced.
  * Looking after bees and each other – with real guardrails, not just vibes.

2026 is our second year at AfrikaBurn. We’re taking what worked in 2025 and making it a little cozier and a lot more intentional.

[ The Crossing ](https://www.afrikaburn.org/project_directory/the-crossing/)
The Crossing consists of two groupings of elements. A rigid line cuts through the landscape, a boundary that may represent law, structure, or control. Intersecting this line are more organic forms that rise irregularly, as though shaped by wind, erosion, or time. The line may be read as a border or a threshold, a boundary between order and chaos, captivity and freedom, life and death. Movement unfolds across this divide. From one perspective, the passage across the line could represent departure or exile, figures leaving something familiar behind. From another, the same movement may suggest arrival, a transition toward possibility and renewal. The Crossing comes together through shared effort, people learning, experimenting, problem-solving, and physically constructing something at scale. It exists because of a community, for a short time, shaped by the desert and the people within it. As with many desert installations, the sculpture is intended to transform through fire. When it ultimately burns, the work completes its own crossing, shifting from structure into flame, light, and ash.
