Participants needed:

🌟 Who We’re Looking For – The Fractal Chill Crew Fractal Chill is a working, gifting-based camp, and we welcome humans who embody contribution, kindness, reliability, and a love for community. We’re looking for people who feel naturally inspired to help create a Bedouin wonderland: tea-makers, coffee lovers, musos, creatives, builders, homestead helpers, and anyone who enjoys being part of a team effort. Ideal crew members are self-motivated, easy-going, and willing to gift at least two hours a day in the Café, Muso, Arty, Builder, or Homestead teams — plus one hour supporting camp hygiene, kitchen care, showers, or MOOP. We value people who show up, participate fully, help during build and strike, and see the camp as a shared home. If you love ceremony, comfort, teamwork, and co-creating magic in the dust, you’ll fit right in with the Fractal Chill family. 

## Project Description:

Fractal Chill is one of AfrikaBurn’s well-known and longstanding camps, continuously refining our gift since 2015. We create a Bedouin-style Ethiopian Tea & Coffee House that offers a daily ritual of comfort, connection, and calm in the dust. Our nomadic tent becomes a soft, carpeted sanctuary where Burners gather for our signature desert tea ceremony – a journey through pineapple-ginger rooibos, Moroccan mint, vanilla black tea, hand-brewed chai, and freshly roasted Ethiopian Arabica coffee.

We seat around 100 people and host open, improvised music jams led by our Music Maister and a roaming crew of troubadours.  
Fractal Chill is a home-from-home, built for rest, ritual, and community. for more visual please refer to our FB page. https://www.facebook.com/share/1XRRrbfswY/?mibextid=wwXIfr

## Details of Gift:

🎁 Our Gift to Tankwa Town – 

Fractal Chill gifts Tankwa Town a Bedouin sanctuary in the dust — a place of warmth, ceremony, and community where anyone can rest, reset, and reconnect. Each day we offer a full desert tea experience: freshly roasted Ethiopian Arabica coffee, hand-brewed chai, Moroccan mint tea, pineapple-ginger rooibos, vanilla black tea, and light snacks such as fruit, biscuits, and popcorn.

Our soft, carpeted lounge and inflatable couches provide rare comfort at the Burn, inviting visitors to sink into a 40-minute ritual that slows the mind and soothes the body.

In the afternoons, our open percussion jam becomes a living gift of sound. Musicians and wanderers drift in, creating spontaneous music that fills the tent and spills into the streets of Tankwa Town.

Our gift is simple but deeply felt: hospitality, ceremony, comfort, and a shared moment of magic in the heart of the desert.

## Joining Requirements:

🛠 Requirements for Joining Fractal Chill To join Fractal Chill, participants must be willing to contribute, collaborate, and help create our Bedouin-style sanctuary in Tankwa Town. Our core requirements are: 1\. Build-Up or Break-Down Participation (Mandatory) All camp members must commit to either Build-Up or Break-Down. Preferably both — but at least one is required. This ensures we honour our site, our gift, and our shared responsibility. 2\. Burners With Experience Preferred (But Not Required) We welcome crew who have burned before, as they understand the rhythm of Tankwa and working camps. However, virgins are absolutely welcome if they bring good energy, a willingness to learn, and a desire to contribute meaningfully. 3\. Daily Gifting Participation All camp members must gift time each day: 2 hours in one of our teams (Café, Muso, Arty, Builder, or Homestead) 1 hour supporting homestead duties (showers, kitchen, MOOP, hygiene) 4\. Being Present & Responsible Crew must be reliable, self-directed, respectful, and ready to show up for the camp and each other.

[ The Crossing ](https://www.afrikaburn.org/project_directory/the-crossing/)

The Crossing consists of two groupings of elements. A rigid line cuts through the landscape, a boundary that may represent law, structure, or control. Intersecting this line are more organic forms that rise irregularly, as though shaped by wind, erosion, or time. The line may be read as a border or a threshold, a boundary between order and chaos, captivity and freedom, life and death. Movement unfolds across this divide. From one perspective, the passage across the line could represent departure or exile, figures leaving something familiar behind. From another, the same movement may suggest arrival, a transition toward possibility and renewal. The Crossing comes together through shared effort, people learning, experimenting, problem-solving, and physically constructing something at scale. It exists because of a community, for a short time, shaped by the desert and the people within it. As with many desert installations, the sculpture is intended to transform through fire. When it ultimately burns, the work completes its own crossing, shifting from structure into flame, light, and ash.
